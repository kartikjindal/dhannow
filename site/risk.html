DHAN NOW Risk Identification, Assessment, Monitoring and Mitigation

Effective Date: [Insert Effective Date] Last Updated: [Insert Last Updated Date]

1. Introduction

DHAN NOW recognises the importance of identifying, assessing, monitoring and managing risks associated with digital loan applications, customer information, technology operations, payment processing, third-party services and customer support.

This Risk Management Policy establishes a framework for identifying material risks, assigning responsibility, implementing appropriate controls and reviewing risk-related incidents.

The policy aims to support business continuity, customer protection, information security, operational reliability and compliance with applicable laws and contractual obligations.

The risk responsibilities of each participating entity depend on its actual legal status and role. Where a regulated bank or Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC) is the actual lender, that lender remains responsible for the credit, lending and regulatory risks assigned to it by applicable law.

2. Objectives

The objectives of this policy are to:

  • Identify material risks arising from business activities.
  • Establish appropriate controls to reduce the likelihood and impact of adverse events.
  • Protect customer information and confidential business records.
  • Reduce fraud, identity misuse and unauthorised transactions.
  • Support reliable technology and payment operations.
  • Monitor third-party service providers.
  • Promote responsible customer treatment.
  • Establish incident reporting and escalation procedures.
  • Support business continuity and recovery from disruptions.
  • Improve risk controls through periodic reviews and corrective actions.

3. Scope

This policy applies, as relevant to each entity's responsibilities, to:

  • DHAN NOW's website and digital customer interface.
  • NAVIX Finance Private Limited, in accordance with its actual role in the business.
  • Directors, management, employees and authorised representatives.
  • Technology, operations and customer support teams.
  • Approved vendors and service providers.
  • Payment and verification service providers.
  • Other participating entities where required by contract or applicable law.

The policy covers risks associated with loan applications, verification, customer data, payment processes, servicing, customer communications, vendor relationships and business operations.

4. Risk Management Principles

DHAN NOW aims to follow these principles:

4.1 Proactive Risk Identification

Risks should be identified before launching a new product, changing a material process, engaging a critical vendor or introducing a significant technology integration.

4.2 Proportionate Controls

Controls should reflect the nature, likelihood and potential impact of the risk.

4.3 Clear Accountability

Each material risk should have an identified owner responsible for monitoring and escalating it.

4.4 Timely Escalation

Significant incidents, control failures and suspected misconduct should be reported promptly to the appropriate authority.

4.5 Continuous Monitoring

Risk assessments should be reviewed periodically and when significant operational, technological or regulatory changes occur.

4.6 Documentation

Material risks, incidents, control decisions and corrective actions should be documented appropriately.

5. Risk Governance and Responsibilities

5.1 Board or Appropriate Governing Authority

The Board of Directors or other legally authorised governing authority of the relevant entity should oversee material risks in accordance with its responsibilities.

Depending on the entity's legal structure, oversight may include:

  • Reviewing significant business risks.
  • Approving risk-related policies where required.
  • Reviewing material incidents and control weaknesses.
  • Monitoring corrective actions.
  • Ensuring appropriate management accountability.

5.2 Senior Management

Senior management should implement the approved risk framework and ensure that relevant teams understand their responsibilities.

Management responsibilities may include:

  • Reviewing operational risk reports.
  • Monitoring significant service-provider issues.
  • Ensuring appropriate access controls.
  • Reviewing customer complaints and recurring incidents.
  • Escalating significant risks.
  • Ensuring corrective actions are completed.

5.3 Risk, Compliance and Operations Teams

The relevant personnel should identify risks within their assigned areas, monitor control effectiveness and report material concerns.

Where a separate risk or compliance department is not established, these responsibilities should be assigned to appropriately authorised personnel.

5.4 Technology and Information Security Teams

The technology function should maintain appropriate system security, monitoring, backup, access management and incident response controls.

5.5 Third-Party Service Providers

Service providers must comply with the applicable contractual, confidentiality, data protection, security and operational requirements relevant to their services.

6. Credit and Underwriting Risk

Credit risk arises when a borrower does not repay amounts due under the applicable loan agreement.

Where DHAN NOW provides a digital application or technology service rather than acting as the actual lender, its role in credit-related activities must remain within the authorised and agreed scope of services.

The actual lender is responsible for making lending decisions and maintaining the credit risk controls required by applicable law.

Credit risk controls may include:

  • Identity verification.
  • Income and affordability assessment.
  • Credit bureau checks, where permitted.
  • Fraud and duplicate-application checks.
  • Review of repayment capacity.
  • Application of approved eligibility criteria.
  • Monitoring of portfolio performance.
  • Review of delinquency and default trends.

Loan approval and loan-limit decisions should be based on the relevant lender's approved underwriting process.

7. Fraud and Identity Misuse Risk

Digital lending services may face risks involving impersonation, false documents, stolen identities, synthetic identities, duplicate applications and unauthorised transactions.

Appropriate controls may include:

  • PAN and permitted identity verification.
  • Mobile number verification.
  • Document authenticity checks.
  • Face matching and liveness checks, where lawfully used.
  • Bank account verification.
  • Device and transaction risk indicators, where appropriately disclosed and permitted.
  • Duplicate application detection.
  • Suspicious activity monitoring.
  • Manual review of flagged applications.

Automated indicators should be used with appropriate oversight and should not replace any human review required by the applicable process.

Suspected fraud should be investigated, documented and escalated through the relevant internal procedure.

8. Operational Risk

Operational risk may arise from human error, inadequate procedures, system failures, inaccurate records, process delays or unauthorised activity.

Controls may include:

  • Documented operating procedures.
  • Defined roles and access permissions.
  • Appropriate approval workflows.
  • Staff training.
  • Reconciliation of relevant transactions.
  • Exception reporting.
  • Recordkeeping.
  • Periodic operational reviews.
  • Corrective action tracking.

Material operational failures should be assessed to identify their cause and reduce the likelihood of recurrence.

9. Technology and Cybersecurity Risk

DHAN NOW recognises that websites, applications, APIs, databases and connected systems may be exposed to cybersecurity threats or technical failures.

Appropriate controls may include:

  • Secure authentication and access management.
  • Encryption where appropriate.
  • Security monitoring and logging.
  • Vulnerability assessment and remediation.
  • Secure software development and change management.
  • API authentication and access controls.
  • Backup and recovery arrangements.
  • Incident detection and response procedures.
  • Periodic security reviews.
  • Restrictions on unauthorised system access.

Security controls should be proportionate to the sensitivity of the information and the criticality of the systems.

Suspected cybersecurity incidents should be reported promptly to the responsible team and handled under the applicable incident response procedure.

10. Data Privacy and Confidentiality Risk

Risks may arise from excessive data collection, unauthorised access, improper disclosure, inadequate retention controls or misuse of customer information.

The responsible entity should maintain appropriate safeguards for:

  • Data collection and use.
  • Customer consent records.
  • Access permissions.
  • Data sharing with authorised vendors.
  • Storage and transmission.
  • Retention and deletion.
  • Privacy requests and complaints.
  • Incident response and legally required notifications.

Personal information must be accessed only for authorised purposes and retained in accordance with applicable law and the relevant retention policy.

11. Payment and Transaction Risk

Payment-related risks may include failed transactions, duplicate payments, incorrect reconciliation, payment fraud, delayed posting and discrepancies between payment records and loan account balances.

Controls may include:

  • Use of approved payment service providers.
  • Secure payment interfaces.
  • Transaction reference tracking.
  • Payment status verification.
  • Reconciliation of payment records.
  • Exception handling.
  • Customer receipts or confirmations.
  • Escalation of disputed transactions.
  • Appropriate access restrictions for payment records.

Customers should be provided with clear instructions for reporting payment issues.

Refunds, reversals and adjustments must be processed in accordance with applicable payment-system rules, contractual arrangements and legal requirements.

12. Third-Party and Vendor Risk

Third-party providers may introduce operational, technology, security, privacy or service-continuity risks.

Before engaging a critical service provider, the responsible entity should undertake appropriate due diligence based on the nature of the service.

Vendor management may include:

  • Review of service capability.
  • Contractual definition of responsibilities.
  • Confidentiality and data protection commitments.
  • Security requirements.
  • Service-level expectations.
  • Incident reporting obligations.
  • Periodic performance reviews.
  • Business continuity arrangements.
  • Exit and transition planning.

Where a regulated lender uses a service provider, the lender must meet its own applicable regulatory responsibilities for outsourcing and oversight.

13. Regulatory and Legal Risk

Regulatory and legal risk may arise from changes in applicable law, incomplete disclosures, inadequate records, non-compliant processes or failure to meet statutory obligations.

The relevant entity should:

  • Identify laws and regulatory requirements applicable to its activities.
  • Monitor material changes.
  • Review customer-facing disclosures and contracts.
  • Maintain required records and approvals.
  • Escalate potential breaches.
  • Implement corrective measures.
  • Obtain legal or compliance advice where appropriate.

The applicability of specific RBI directions or other regulatory requirements depends on the legal status and activities of the relevant entity.

14. Customer Conduct and Reputational Risk

Customer complaints, misleading disclosures, poor service or inappropriate collection practices may damage customer trust and create legal or reputational exposure.

Risk controls should include:

  • Accurate product information.
  • Clear disclosure of charges and repayment obligations.
  • Professional customer communications.
  • Accessible grievance channels.
  • Monitoring of recurring complaints.
  • Appropriate oversight of authorised representatives.
  • Timely correction of errors.
  • Fair and respectful collection practices.

Customer-facing personnel must not promise guaranteed approval, misrepresent the lender or make misleading statements about loan terms.

15. Interest Rate, Pricing and Disclosure Risk

Incorrect pricing information or inconsistencies between website content and loan documents can create customer disputes and compliance risks.

Before publishing or changing loan pricing information, the responsible team should verify:

  • Processing fee.
  • Applicable taxes.
  • Interest rate and calculation method.
  • Tenure.
  • Net disbursal.
  • Repayment amount.
  • Applicable APR.
  • Late-payment charges, if any.
  • Extension or restructuring terms, where offered.

For the stated DHAN NOW 14-day product, the pricing structure is a 10% processing fee, GST at 18% of the processing fee and interest of 1% per day for 14 calendar days on the original principal.

The displayed figures must be consistent with the customer's actual offer and loan documents.

APR must be calculated and disclosed separately according to the applicable prescribed methodology and actual loan cash flows.

16. Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery

The responsible entity should maintain suitable arrangements to minimise disruption from system failures, cyber incidents, infrastructure outages, vendor failures or other significant events.

Depending on operational requirements, continuity arrangements may include:

  • System backups.
  • Data recovery procedures.
  • Alternative communication channels.
  • Vendor escalation contacts.
  • Incident response responsibilities.
  • Recovery priorities for critical services.
  • Periodic testing of recovery procedures.
  • Documentation of lessons learned.

Material disruptions should be communicated to affected customers where appropriate and in accordance with applicable requirements.

17. Incident Reporting and Escalation

Employees and authorised service providers should promptly report material incidents through the designated internal channel.

Reportable events may include:

  • Suspected fraud.
  • Data leakage or unauthorised disclosure.
  • Cybersecurity incidents.
  • Material service outages.
  • Significant payment discrepancies.
  • Unauthorised access or transactions.
  • Serious customer complaints.
  • Vendor failures affecting critical services.
  • Potential legal or regulatory breaches.

The responsible team should document the incident, assess its impact, take appropriate containment measures and escalate it to the relevant authority.

Any external notification must be made in accordance with applicable legal and regulatory requirements.

18. Risk Assessment and Monitoring

Risk assessments should be conducted periodically and when significant changes occur.

The assessment may consider:

  • Nature of the risk.
  • Likelihood of occurrence.
  • Potential financial or operational impact.
  • Impact on customers.
  • Existing controls.
  • Remaining exposure.
  • Required corrective actions.
  • Responsible person or team.
  • Target completion date.

Material risks and overdue corrective actions should be escalated to the appropriate management or governing authority.

19. Recordkeeping and Documentation

Appropriate records should be maintained for material risk assessments, incidents, investigations, control reviews and corrective actions.

Records should be protected against unauthorised access or alteration and retained in accordance with applicable legal and regulatory requirements.

20. Policy Review and Approval

This policy should be reviewed periodically and whenever significant changes occur in the business model, technology environment, risk profile or applicable legal requirements.

17. INTEREST RATE POLICY

DHAN NOW Interest Calculation, Processing Fees, Applicable Taxes and Customer Disclosures

Effective Date: [Insert Effective Date] Last Updated: [Insert Last Updated Date]

1. Introduction

DHAN NOW is committed to providing customers with clear and transparent information about loan interest, processing fees, applicable taxes, repayment obligations and other charges associated with its digital loan products.

This Interest Rate Policy explains the stated pricing structure for the DHAN NOW short-term loan product and the principles governing the disclosure of interest and charges.

The actual lender must determine, approve and apply the interest rate and applicable charges in accordance with its lending arrangements, applicable law and regulatory requirements. The pricing displayed to customers must be consistent with the applicable Key Facts Statement (KFS), sanction communication and loan agreement.

2. Purpose of the Policy

The objectives of this policy are to:

  • Explain the applicable interest calculation method.
  • Disclose the processing fee and applicable GST.
  • Explain upfront deductions and net disbursal.
  • Clarify the scheduled repayment amount and due date.
  • Promote transparency in loan pricing.
  • Ensure that customers receive applicable pricing information before accepting a loan.
  • Establish consistent principles for pricing disclosures.
  • Support compliance with applicable legal and regulatory requirements.

3. Loan Product Details

The standard DHAN NOW short-term loan product follows the structure below:

4. Interest Rate and Calculation Method

The stated interest rate is 1% per day, calculated on the original loan principal for the standard 14-calendar-day tenure.

Under this structure, the interest for the full 14-day period is calculated as follows:

Interest = Original Principal × 1% × 14 Days

Accordingly:

Interest for 14 days = 14% of the original principal.

This calculation uses simple interest on the original principal. It does not apply daily compounding.

The interest amount and applicable repayment terms must be disclosed to the customer before acceptance of the loan offer.

5. Processing Fee

The processing fee is calculated at 10% of the original loan principal.

Processing Fee = Original Principal × 10%

The applicable processing fee must be disclosed before the customer accepts the loan.

Any processing fee charged must be consistent with the applicable loan documents and the actual lender's approved pricing arrangements.

6. Goods and Services Tax (GST)

GST is calculated at 18% of the processing fee under the stated pricing structure.

GST = Processing Fee × 18%

For example, where the loan principal is ₹1,000:

  • Processing fee: ₹100.
  • GST at 18% of the processing fee: ₹18.

The applicable tax treatment must follow the relevant tax laws and the nature of the service being supplied.

7. Upfront Deduction and Net Disbursal

Under the stated product structure, the processing fee, applicable GST and 14-day interest are deducted from the original principal before the net loan amount is disbursed.

Total Upfront Deductions = Processing Fee + GST + 14-Day Interest

Net Disbursal = Original Principal − Total Upfront Deductions

The customer must be informed of the sanctioned principal, each applicable deduction and the resulting net disbursal before accepting the loan offer.

9. Repayment Due Date

The standard repayment due date is the date specified in the customer's loan documents, based on the agreed 14-calendar-day tenure.

The customer must be informed of the actual due date before accepting the loan.

The repayment schedule in the KFS and loan agreement will govern if it contains the applicable customer-specific dates and terms.

Customers should repay through the approved payment channels and retain the payment confirmation or transaction reference.

10. Extension or Rollover

Where a 14-day extension or rollover option is available and permitted under the applicable lending arrangement, the customer must receive a separate disclosure of the revised terms before accepting the option.

Under the stated pricing formula, the additional 14-day period is calculated using:

  • Processing fee: 10% of the applicable principal.
  • GST: 18% of the processing fee.
  • Interest: 1% per day for 14 days on the original principal used for the calculation.

For each ₹1,000 of principal used in the calculation, these components total ₹258.

An extension or rollover is not automatic. It may be offered only through the approved process, subject to the applicable lender's approval and customer acceptance.

The customer must be informed of the additional amount payable, revised due date and total repayment obligation before accepting the extension.

Any processing fee, GST or other charge applied to an extension must be legally permissible and accurately disclosed in the relevant documents.

11. EMI Conversion

Where an EMI-conversion option is available, the customer must receive the revised repayment terms before accepting the change.

Under the stated 20-day EMI-conversion calculation:

  • Interest is calculated at 1% per day for 20 days on the original principal.
  • Total interest equals 20% of the original principal.
  • Total repayment equals the original principal plus the calculated interest.
  • The total amount is divided into two equal instalments.

12. Annual Percentage Rate (APR) Disclosure

The Annual Percentage Rate (APR) represents the annualised cost of credit calculated using the prescribed methodology and applicable loan cash flows.

APR is not necessarily equal to the nominal interest rate multiplied by the number of days in the loan period.

Where required, the applicable APR must be disclosed to the customer in the KFS and other relevant loan disclosures before acceptance.

The APR calculation should reflect the applicable cash flows and charges in accordance with the relevant regulatory methodology.

The 14% interest calculated for the standard 14-day period must not be described as the APR.

13. Late Payment and Other Charges

Any late-payment charge, penal charge or other applicable charge must be permitted under the relevant lending arrangement and applicable law.

Before the customer accepts the loan, applicable charges must be disclosed in the relevant documents.

Charges must not be imposed in a manner inconsistent with the loan agreement, applicable regulatory directions or customer disclosures.

Where a charge applies, the customer should be able to understand the event that triggers the charge and how the amount is determined.

14. No Undisclosed Charges

Customers must be informed of the applicable loan pricing before accepting the loan.

The relevant lender and authorised service providers must not impose undisclosed charges contrary to applicable law or the agreed loan terms.

Where a change in pricing or repayment terms is permitted, it must be implemented through the required approval, disclosure and acceptance process.

15. Customer Disclosure and Acceptance

Before accepting a loan offer, the customer should be able to review:

  • Sanctioned principal.
  • Processing fee.
  • Applicable GST.
  • Interest rate and calculation method.
  • Tenure and due date.
  • Total upfront deductions.
  • Net disbursal amount.
  • Total repayment obligation.
  • APR, where required.
  • Late-payment charges and other applicable fees.
  • Available extension or EMI-conversion options, where offered.
  • Identity of the actual lender.
  • Key Facts Statement and loan agreement.

The customer must complete the applicable acceptance process before the loan is disbursed.

16. Responsibilities of the Actual Lender

The actual lender is responsible for the lending decisions, pricing approvals, applicable regulatory disclosures and other responsibilities assigned to it under law.

DHAN NOW's role and the role of NAVIX Finance Private Limited must be described accurately according to the actual lending and servicing arrangements.

The applicable KFS, sanction communication and loan agreement must identify the actual lender and specify the relevant loan terms.

17. Policy Review

This policy will be reviewed periodically and whenever a material change occurs in the loan product, pricing structure, business arrangements or applicable regulatory requirements.

Any revised pricing must be approved by the appropriate authority and reflected consistently in the website, calculator, KFS, sanction communication and loan agreement.
