DHAN NOW Governance, Accountability, Risk Oversight and Ethical Business Conduct

Effective Date: [Insert Effective Date] Last Updated: [Insert Last Updated Date]

1. Introduction

DHAN NOW is committed to maintaining appropriate standards of corporate governance, transparency, accountability, ethical conduct and responsible business management.

This Corporate Governance Policy establishes the principles and framework for responsible decision-making, risk oversight, regulatory compliance, protection of customer information and monitoring of business operations associated with the DHAN NOW platform.

The policy is intended to promote clear responsibilities, appropriate internal controls, fair customer treatment and accountability across the organisation and its authorised service providers.

The legal responsibilities of each participating entity must be determined by its actual legal status, contractual obligations and applicable laws. Where a regulated bank or Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC) is the actual lender, that lender remains responsible for its own statutory and regulatory obligations.

2. Purpose and Objectives

The objectives of this policy are to:

  • Establish clear governance responsibilities.
  • Promote ethical and transparent business practices.
  • Support compliance with applicable laws and regulations.
  • Maintain appropriate financial, operational and technology controls.
  • Protect customer interests and confidential information.
  • Monitor business risks and operational performance.
  • Establish accountability for decisions and business activities.
  • Support effective complaint handling and corrective action.
  • Oversee third-party service providers and contractual arrangements.
  • Encourage continuous improvement in governance practices.

3. Scope and Applicability

This policy applies, as relevant to each entity's role, to:

  • DHAN NOW as the customer-facing brand and digital platform.
  • NAVIX Finance Private Limited, where acting in the capacity applicable to the relevant transaction.
  • Directors and senior management of the relevant legal entity.
  • Employees and authorised representatives.
  • Technology and operational teams.
  • Customer support and grievance personnel.
  • Third-party vendors and service providers.
  • Any other participating entity to the extent required by its contractual or legal responsibilities.

The policy must be implemented in a manner consistent with the actual legal and operational structure of the business.

Where an activity is the responsibility of the actual lender or another regulated entity, this policy does not transfer or override that entity's statutory responsibilities.

4. Governance Principles

DHAN NOW aims to follow these core principles:

4.1 Transparency

Material information concerning loan products, applicable charges, customer obligations, complaints and relevant business processes should be communicated accurately and clearly.

4.2 Accountability

Responsibilities should be assigned to appropriate individuals or teams. Decisions and material business activities should be supported by suitable records.

4.3 Integrity

Employees and authorised representatives must act honestly and professionally and must not engage in fraud, deception, bribery, corruption or other improper conduct.

4.4 Customer Protection

Customer interests, privacy, fair treatment and access to grievance channels should be considered when developing and operating products and services.

4.5 Compliance

Applicable laws, regulatory directions, contractual obligations and approved internal policies must be identified and followed.

4.6 Risk Management

Material business, credit-related, operational, technology, cybersecurity, privacy and third-party risks should be identified, assessed and monitored by the responsible entity.

4.7 Continuous Improvement

Governance arrangements should be reviewed periodically and improved when operational experience, complaints, audit findings or regulatory changes indicate a need.

5. Roles and Responsibilities

5.1 Board of Directors

The Board of Directors of the relevant legal entity will exercise oversight in accordance with its statutory responsibilities and applicable law.

Depending on the entity's legal structure and regulatory status, the Board's responsibilities may include:

  • Approving applicable business strategies and policies.
  • Reviewing material business and operational risks.
  • Monitoring compliance and internal control arrangements.
  • Reviewing significant incidents and corrective actions.
  • Ensuring appropriate accountability at management level.
  • Overseeing related-party transactions and conflicts of interest, where applicable.
  • Reviewing governance arrangements at appropriate intervals.

Where a specific matter is legally reserved for the Board, it must be approved through the required governance process.

5.2 Senior Management

Senior management is responsible for implementing approved business plans, policies and internal controls.

Responsibilities may include:

  • Monitoring daily business operations.
  • Ensuring appropriate staffing and operational supervision.
  • Maintaining accurate records and reporting.
  • Monitoring service-provider performance.
  • Addressing customer complaints and recurring service issues.
  • Escalating material incidents and compliance concerns.
  • Implementing corrective actions approved by the appropriate authority.

5.3 Compliance and Legal Functions

The relevant compliance or legal function, where established, will support the identification and monitoring of applicable legal and regulatory requirements.

Its responsibilities may include:

  • Reviewing customer-facing disclosures and contractual documents.
  • Monitoring regulatory developments.
  • Maintaining a record of applicable compliance obligations.
  • Advising on regulatory or contractual concerns.
  • Coordinating responses to regulatory or legal notices.
  • Escalating material compliance issues to authorised management.

These responsibilities must be allocated in accordance with the actual structure and requirements of the relevant entity.

5.4 Technology and Information Security

The responsible technology and information security teams should maintain appropriate controls for the systems used to support DHAN NOW's services.

These may include:

  • Access management.
  • Authentication and authorisation controls.
  • Secure handling of customer information.
  • System monitoring and incident reporting.
  • Backup and recovery arrangements.
  • Vulnerability management.
  • Vendor and integration security reviews.
  • Business continuity and disaster recovery arrangements.

5.5 Customer Support and Grievance Handling

Customer-facing personnel should provide accurate information, follow approved procedures and handle complaints respectfully.

They must not make unauthorised promises about loan approval, interest rates, repayment extensions, refunds or other matters.

Complaints should be recorded and escalated in accordance with the applicable grievance redressal procedure.

6. Regulatory and Legal Compliance

The relevant entity must identify and comply with the laws and regulations applicable to its activities.

Depending on the actual business structure, these may include requirements relating to:

  • Companies Act and applicable corporate filings.
  • Reserve Bank of India requirements applicable to regulated lenders.
  • Digital lending arrangements.
  • Customer disclosures and fair practices.
  • Know Your Customer and anti-money laundering obligations, where applicable.
  • Personal data protection and privacy.
  • Payment systems and electronic mandates.
  • Information security and cybersecurity.
  • Consumer protection.
  • Employment and contractual obligations.
  • Taxation and statutory accounting.

The applicability of a specific law or regulatory direction depends on the entity's legal status, activities and role in the relevant transaction.

No statement on the DHAN NOW website should imply that a company is an RBI-regulated NBFC unless that status has been established and is accurately disclosed.

7. Ethical Business Conduct

Directors, employees and authorised representatives must act in good faith and in the legitimate interests of the relevant entity.

The following conduct is prohibited:

  • Fraud, bribery or corruption.
  • Falsification of business or customer records.
  • Misuse of confidential information.
  • Unauthorised access to systems or customer data.
  • Misleading statements to customers or business partners.
  • Concealment of material conflicts of interest.
  • Improper inducements or unauthorised payments.
  • Retaliation against individuals who raise genuine concerns.
  • Unlawful discrimination or harassment.

Suspected misconduct should be reported through an appropriate internal channel and reviewed fairly.

8. Conflict of Interest Management

A conflict of interest may arise where a person's private interests, financial interests or relationships could improperly influence a business decision.

Directors, officers and employees should disclose material conflicts in accordance with applicable law and internal procedures.

Where required, the relevant person should abstain from participating in the decision until the conflict has been reviewed and managed.

Related-party transactions must be identified, documented, reviewed and approved in accordance with applicable law and the governance requirements of the relevant entity.

9. Risk Management and Internal Controls

The relevant entity should maintain controls appropriate to the size, nature and complexity of its operations.

Risk management may cover:

  • Operational risk.
  • Technology and cybersecurity risk.
  • Data privacy risk.
  • Fraud and identity misuse.
  • Third-party and vendor risk.
  • Service interruption.
  • Financial and settlement risk.
  • Compliance and legal risk.
  • Customer complaint and conduct risk.
  • Business continuity risk.

Material risks should be escalated to the appropriate management or governance authority.

Where an actual lender is responsible for credit underwriting, lending exposure and related regulated activities, the applicable credit and regulatory risk responsibilities must remain with that lender in accordance with law and the lending arrangement.

10. Customer Data Governance

Customer information must be handled in accordance with the applicable privacy notice, customer consents, contractual arrangements and legal requirements.

The responsible entity should maintain appropriate controls relating to:

  • Collection and use of personal information.
  • Access permissions.
  • Data sharing with authorised service providers.
  • Retention and deletion.
  • Security monitoring.
  • Incident management.
  • Handling of privacy requests and complaints.

Employees and vendors must access customer information only where authorised and necessary for their assigned responsibilities.

11. Third-Party and Vendor Governance

Where technology providers, verification agencies, payment service providers, communication vendors or other third parties support the business, the relevant entity should establish appropriate selection, contractual and monitoring processes.

Depending on the nature of the service, these processes may include:

  • Due diligence before engagement.
  • Defined scope of services and responsibilities.
  • Confidentiality and data protection obligations.
  • Information security requirements.
  • Service-level expectations.
  • Incident reporting requirements.
  • Audit or assurance rights where appropriate.
  • Business continuity requirements.
  • Exit and data-return arrangements.

Outsourcing a service does not remove any responsibility that applicable law places on the entity engaging the service provider.

12. Financial and Recordkeeping Controls

The relevant legal entity must maintain financial and corporate records in accordance with applicable law and accounting requirements.

Appropriate controls should support:

  • Accurate transaction records.
  • Proper authorisation of payments.
  • Reconciliation of relevant accounts.
  • Timely statutory filings.
  • Preservation of contracts and approvals.
  • Documented financial reporting.
  • Prevention and detection of unauthorised transactions.

Financial and corporate records must not be altered, concealed or destroyed contrary to applicable retention requirements.

13. Complaint Monitoring and Corrective Action

Customer complaints should be monitored to identify recurring problems, service deficiencies, potential misconduct or weaknesses in internal controls.

Where a significant issue is identified, the responsible team should assess its cause and implement appropriate corrective action.

Material incidents and repeated complaints should be escalated to the relevant management or governance authority where warranted.

Complaint trends may be reviewed periodically to improve service quality, disclosure practices and customer experience.

14. Internal Review and Audit

The relevant entity should conduct internal reviews appropriate to its operations, risk profile and legal obligations.

Such reviews may cover:

  • Compliance with approved policies.
  • Accuracy of customer-facing disclosures.
  • Access controls and information security.
  • Vendor performance.
  • Complaint handling.
  • Transaction and reconciliation controls.
  • Recordkeeping.
  • Implementation of corrective actions.

Where an audit is legally required, it must be conducted by appropriately qualified and authorised persons in accordance with the applicable requirements.

Findings should be documented and material issues escalated to the appropriate authority.

15. Reporting and Escalation of Material Incidents

Material incidents should be reported promptly through the designated internal process.

Examples may include:

  • Suspected fraud.
  • Unauthorised access to customer information.
  • Significant service disruption.
  • Material payment-processing issues.
  • Serious customer complaints.
  • Regulatory notices or legal proceedings.
  • Suspected employee misconduct.
  • Significant vendor or security incidents.

The responsible entity should assess the incident, preserve relevant records, take appropriate corrective measures and make any notification required by applicable law.

16. Whistleblowing and Reporting Concerns

Employees and authorised representatives should have an appropriate way to report suspected fraud, corruption, misconduct, data misuse or regulatory non-compliance.

Reports should be handled fairly and confidentially to the extent practicable and permitted by law.

Retaliation against a person for raising a genuine concern in good faith is not acceptable.

Where a formal whistleblower mechanism is required by law, the relevant entity must maintain and operate it in accordance with the applicable requirements.

17. Policy Approval and Review

This policy should be approved by the appropriate authority of the relevant legal entity.

The policy should be reviewed periodically and whenever material changes occur in the business structure, regulatory requirements, technology systems or risk profile.

Updates should be documented and communicated to the personnel and service providers to whom they apply.
