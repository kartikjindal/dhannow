1. Purpose of This Policy

DHAN NOW is committed to ensuring that customers can access, review, accept, and retain their loan documentation through the applicable authorised digital process.

This Loan Agreement Access Policy explains how customers may access their loan agreement and related documents, understand the applicable terms, complete the required acceptance process, and request assistance when a document is unavailable or appears incorrect.

It must be read together with the applicable Terms & Conditions, Privacy Policy, Customer Consent Information, Loan Information & Repayment Policy, and KFS Information page.

3. What Is a Loan Agreement?

A loan agreement is a legally relevant document that records the terms and conditions governing a loan between the borrower and the actual lender.

Depending on the loan product and applicable requirements, it may contain:

  • The borrower's details.
  • The actual lender's legal name and details.
  • The approved principal amount.
  • The loan tenure.
  • The applicable interest rate and calculation method.
  • Processing fees and applicable taxes.
  • Annual Percentage Rate (APR).
  • The net amount to be disbursed.
  • Repayment amounts and due dates.
  • Applicable late-payment or penal charges.
  • Repayment and payment methods.
  • Terms relating to early repayment.
  • Applicable cancellation or cooling-off provisions.
  • Default and recovery provisions.
  • Privacy and data-processing disclosures, where applicable.
  • Customer grievance redressal details.
  • Other contractual terms required for the loan.

The exact contents depend on the applicable loan product, the lender's documentation, and legal and regulatory requirements.

4. Accessing the Loan Agreement

Customers should be provided access to the applicable loan agreement through the authorised application journey or another permitted channel.

Depending on the system implemented, the agreement may be made available through:

  • The loan application interface.
  • A secure customer portal.
  • An authenticated document-access link.
  • Email or another authorised electronic channel.
  • Another method approved by the actual lender and permitted by applicable law.

Customers may be required to complete identity verification or authentication before accessing confidential loan documents.

Access controls should be designed to prevent unauthorised individuals from viewing another customer's loan agreement.

Website implementation requirement: The live DHAN NOW platform should provide a clear and functional method for eligible customers to access their loan agreement.

5. When Should the Customer Receive the Agreement?

The loan agreement and required disclosures must be provided at the stage and in the manner prescribed by applicable law and the relevant lending process.

Customers should be given an appropriate opportunity to review the applicable loan terms before accepting the loan.

Where applicable, the lender must provide the KFS and other required documents before the customer enters into the loan contract.

The document presented for acceptance must correspond to the actual loan offer, including the approved amount, tenure, charges, repayment schedule, and other material terms.

A general website description or loan calculator is not a substitute for the customer's actual loan agreement.

6. Reviewing the Agreement Before Acceptance

Before accepting a loan agreement, customers should carefully review the following information:

  • Actual lender's legal name.
  • Approved loan amount.
  • Net amount to be disbursed.
  • Interest rate and calculation method.
  • Processing fee and applicable taxes.
  • APR.
  • Loan tenure and repayment due date.
  • Instalment amounts, where applicable.
  • Applicable penal charges.
  • Early repayment and foreclosure terms, where applicable.
  • Cooling-off or look-up period and exit procedure, where applicable.
  • Payment and electronic mandate arrangements.
  • Privacy and consent disclosures.
  • Customer support and grievance redressal details.
  • Other contractual obligations.

If any material term is unclear or inconsistent with the offer presented, customers should request clarification before accepting the agreement.

7. Electronic Acceptance and Execution

Where permitted by applicable law, a loan agreement may be accepted or executed electronically through an authorised process.

Depending on the actual system, the process may involve:

  • Reviewing the agreement electronically.
  • Confirming acceptance of the displayed terms.
  • Completing the prescribed electronic-signature process.
  • Completing an authorised authentication step.
  • Receiving an electronic confirmation or executed copy.

The actual acceptance method must comply with applicable law and the lender's approved procedures.

A customer should not be treated as having accepted a loan merely because the customer opened the website, started an application, or viewed the agreement.

Required consent and execution records must be maintained in accordance with applicable legal, regulatory, and contractual requirements.

8. Electronic Signatures and Authentication

Where electronic signatures or other electronic execution methods are used, the process must meet the applicable legal requirements.

The system should clearly identify the document being accepted and provide the customer with the required information before the execution step.

Authentication methods must be used only for their intended and authorised purpose.

If a customer suspects that a loan agreement has been executed without their authorisation, the customer should promptly notify the actual lender through its official support or grievance channel.

11. Incorrect, Incomplete, or Unavailable Documents

Customers should promptly report a loan agreement or related document that appears to contain:

  • An incorrect loan amount.
  • An incorrect interest rate.
  • Incorrect fees or taxes.
  • An incorrect repayment schedule.
  • An incorrect lender name.
  • Missing pages or incomplete information.
  • A document belonging to another person.
  • An incorrect or unauthorised acceptance record.
  • Another material discrepancy.

Customers may be asked to provide their registered mobile number, loan reference, document date, and a description of the issue.

Sensitive authentication credentials should not be included in the complaint.

The responsible team should review the matter and, where appropriate, coordinate with the actual lender to correct an error, provide the appropriate document, or investigate the issue.

No material contractual change should be made without following the applicable legal and contractual process.

Where required, the customer should receive an amended agreement, revised schedule, or other appropriate documentation.

Customers should retain copies of any amendments or revised repayment terms.

An extension, restructuring, instalment conversion, or other change to a loan must be documented through the applicable authorised process.

13. Loan Agreement and Repayment Obligations

The executed loan agreement, applicable KFS, and repayment schedule should be read together to understand the customer's repayment obligations.

Customers should verify the amount payable and the relevant due date before making a repayment.

If a payment has been made but is not reflected in the loan account, the customer should retain the transaction reference and report the issue promptly.

A document-access request or complaint does not automatically suspend the customer's repayment obligations. Any change to a due date, repayment arrangement, or contractual obligation must be handled in accordance with the applicable agreement, law, and the actual lender's authorised process.

This provision does not restrict any customer right or protection available under applicable law.
